DVI TMDS Differential Pair Trace Length Mismatch

In high-speed PCB design for DVI (Digital Visual Interface), controlling trace length mismatch within Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) pairs is critical for maintaining signal integrity and minimizing jitter. This guide details the allowable trace length mismatch within individual differential pairs (intra-pair skew), contrasts it with pair-to-pair limits (inter-pair skew), and outlines practical routing rules required to comply with the DVI 1.0 standard.

Intra-Pair Trace Length Limits

The allowable trace length mismatch within a single DVI TMDS differential pair (intra-pair skew) is strictly limited to 5 mils (0.127 mm) for optimal performance, with an absolute maximum allowable tolerance of 10 mils (0.254 mm).

DVI single-link connections operate at pixel clock rates up to 165 MHz, which translates to a serialized data rate of up to 1.65 Gbps per channel. At this speed, a single bit period (\(T_{\text{bit}}\)) is approximately 606 picoseconds. The DVI 1.0 specification defines an allowable transmitter intra-pair skew of no more than \(0.15 \times T_{\text{bit}}\) (roughly 90 picoseconds). Because the silicon transmitter package, external connectors, and the DVI cable consume the vast majority of this timing budget, the PCB trace layout must introduce negligible skew—typically less than 1 to 2 picoseconds. In standard FR-4 dielectric substrates, electrical signals travel at roughly 6 to 7 inches per nanosecond; keeping the length delta below 5 mils ensures the PCB adds less than 0.8 picoseconds of skew.

Inter-Pair Trace Length Limits

While intra-pair matching must be extremely tight, the tolerance between different TMDS pairs (inter-pair skew)—such as Data0 to Data1, or Data to Clock—is much more relaxed:

  • Maximum Inter-Pair Mismatch: Typically restricted to within 50 mils to 100 mils (1.27 mm to 2.54 mm).

The DVI specification permits up to \(0.4 \times T_{\text{character}}\) (where character period is \(10 \times T_{\text{bit}}\)) of inter-pair skew across the channel, allowing the receiver to re-synchronize parallel data channels using the dedicated TMDS clock. Maintaining inter-pair trace lengths within 50 mils avoids excessive phase variance between data lanes.

Critical Routing Rules for TMDS Pairs

To meet these strict tolerances on a printed circuit board, implement the following design practices:

  • Tuning Location: Add accordion (meander) tuning as close as possible to the source of the mismatch—typically right where traces break out from an asymmetrical pinout or BGA package.
  • Controlled Impedance: Maintain a 100-ohm differential impedance (or 50-ohm single-ended impedance) along the entire run.
  • Reference Planes: Route TMDS lines over an uninterrupted, solid ground reference plane. Never route differential traces over splits, gaps, or across different power domains.
  • Symmetrical Vias: If layer transitions are required, transition both signals of the differential pair simultaneously using identical via structures, and place nearby ground return vias to maintain return-path continuity.
