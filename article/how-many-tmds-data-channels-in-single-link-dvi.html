How Many TMDS Data Channels in Single-Link DVI?

A single-link DVI cable utilizes exactly three Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) data channels alongside one dedicated TMDS clock channel. These three physical twisted-pair data links transmit the primary red, green, and blue color components of the digital video signal. By dedicating one channel per primary color, the single-link configuration achieves a maximum pixel clock rate of 165 MHz, sufficient to drive standard high-definition displays up to 1920×1200 resolution at 60 Hz.

The Architecture of TMDS

TMDS stands for Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling, an encoding and transmission standard developed by Silicon Image. Its primary design goal is to carry high-speed serial data over copper conductors while reducing electromagnetic interference (EMI) and maintaining data integrity over cable lengths.

TMDS works by applying an 8b/10b encoding algorithm that minimizes the total number of voltage transitions (0-to-1 or 1-to-0). Fewer transitions reduce high-frequency radiation, while differential signaling—sending the true signal alongside an inverted copy across a twisted pair—cancels out external electrical noise at the receiving end.

Channel Allocation in Single-Link DVI

In any DVI connection, signals are broken down into discrete differential pairs. For single-link DVI (found in DVI-D Single Link and DVI-I Single Link connectors), the transmission architecture consists of four active differential pairs in total:

  • TMDS Data Channel 0: Carries the Blue color component, as well as the horizontal synchronization (HSync) and vertical synchronization (VSync) signals during video blanking intervals.
  • TMDS Data Channel 1: Transmits the Green color component and secondary control data.
  • TMDS Data Channel 2: Transmits the Red color component and control data.
  • TMDS Clock Channel: Provides a stable frequency reference to allow the receiver to synchronize and correctly sample incoming bits across all three data channels.

While four physical differential pairs exist, only three function as active data channels for carrying pixel payload.

Bandwidth and Resolution Limits

The three-channel limit directly defines the operational bandwidth of single-link DVI. Each data channel can handle 10 bits of data per pixel clock cycle. Operating at the standard maximum pixel clock of 165 MHz:

  • Per-channel throughput: 1.65 Gbps (165 MHz × 10 bits)
  • Total raw data bandwidth: 4.95 Gbps across all three data channels (3 × 1.65 Gbps)
  • Effective video payload: 3.96 Gbps (accounting for 8b/10b encoding overhead)

Because of this three-channel architecture, single-link DVI tops out at 1920×1080 (1080p) or 1920×1200 at standard 60 Hz refresh rates. Handling higher resolutions such as 2560×1600 or 144 Hz refresh rates requires dual-link DVI, which doubles the architecture by utilizing six TMDS data channels over the same interface.
