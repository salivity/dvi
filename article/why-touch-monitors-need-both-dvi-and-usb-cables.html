Why Touch Monitors Need Both DVI and USB Cables

Touchscreen monitors require both a DVI cable and a separate USB cable because a touch display functions as two distinct hardware devices housed in a single chassis: a visual output display and a touch input device. The DVI cable is dedicated entirely to transmitting the visual video feed from the computer to the monitor screen, while the USB cable transmits touch interaction data—such as taps, swipes, and coordinates—back from the monitor to the computer's operating system.

DVI Is a Video-Only Interface

Digital Visual Interface (DVI) was designed strictly as a video transmission standard. Its architecture consists of channels dedicated to sending high-bandwidth digital display signals from a computer's graphics processing unit (GPU) to the monitor.

Because DVI is fundamentally a unidirectional display interface, it lacks the hardware lines, protocol, and bandwidth management required to carry peripheral data. It cannot send commands, mouse inputs, or touch coordinates back to the host computer.

The Touchscreen Acts as an Input Device

The touch-sensing capability of a monitor relies on an integrated digitizer overlay, which operates independently of the LCD panel underneath. To your operating system, this digitizer is recognized as a Human Interface Device (HID), identical in function to a standard USB mouse, trackpad, or drawing tablet.

To make the touch functionality work:

  • The digitizer detects physical contact on the glass.
  • An internal touch controller calculates the exact X and Y coordinates of the touch point.
  • The USB cable transmits this coordinate and click data to the computer in real time.

Without the USB cable, the monitor can still display your desktop via the DVI connection, but physical touches on the screen will not register.

Why Modern Interfaces Differ

Modern standards like USB-C and Thunderbolt integrate video signals (via DisplayPort Alternate Mode), bidirectional data transfer, and power delivery into a single connector. However, legacy and dedicated display connections like DVI, VGA, and standard DisplayPort/HDMI were built primarily for media playback. As a result, any touch monitor utilizing DVI must rely on an auxiliary USB cable to bridge the communication gap between the touch digitizer and the operating system.
