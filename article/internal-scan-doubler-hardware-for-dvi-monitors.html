Internal Scan Doubler Hardware for DVI Monitors

This article explores the internal scan-doubler hardware designed to connect retro computers to modern DVI monitors. Classic computers generate low-frequency 15 kHz video signals that modern digital displays cannot natively interpret. By installing dedicated internal scan doublers—most notably Individual Computers' Indivision series—users can bridge this generational gap. The following sections outline the technology behind these hardware devices, the prominent models available, and the operational benefits they bring to classic computing setups.

The 15 kHz Display Problem

Classic microcomputers from the 1980s and 1990s, such as the Commodore Amiga and Atari ST, natively output analog video at horizontal scan rates around 15 kHz to match standard CRT televisions. Modern digital monitors using DVI or HDMI typically require a minimum horizontal frequency of 31 kHz. Without hardware intervention, connecting these legacy systems to flat-panel displays results in an "out of range" or "no signal" error.

Primary Hardware Solution: The Indivision Series

The leading dedicated internal hardware solution for bridging this gap directly to DVI is the Indivision line of scan doublers and flicker fixers, engineered by Individual Computers (iComp).

  • Indivision AGA MK2 / MK2 CR: Specifically built for the Amiga 1200 and Amiga 4000 series, this board clips directly over the computer's native video chip (the Lisa chip in the A1200 or the custom display circuitry in the A4000). It features an integrated DVI-I connector that routes out of the computer case, delivering both digital (DVI-D) and analog (VGA via adapter) signals.
  • Indivision ECS: Designed for systems utilizing the Enhanced Chip Set or Original Chip Set (such as the Amiga 500, 600, 2000, and 3000), this board seats directly into or under the Denise chip socket. While earlier revisions targeted VGA, companion adapters and later iterations route native digital signals directly compatible with DVI/HDMI displays.

How Internal Scan Doublers Function

Unlike external composite-to-HDMI/DVI upscalers that suffer from latency and signal degradation, internal scan doublers capture video data directly from the system bus:

  1. Digital Bus Sniffing: The hardware captures the raw pixel stream directly from the system's graphics chips before the video data reaches the native digital-to-analog converter (DAC).
  2. Line Doubling and Frame Buffering: On-board FPGA (Field-Programmable Gate Array) logic doubles each horizontal line, effectively boosting the 15 kHz signal up to the standard 31 kHz or higher required by modern displays.
  3. Flicker Fixing: Interlaced display modes (such as standard PAL/NTSC High-Res Interlaced modes) are de-interlaced in the hardware buffer, eliminating the severe screen flicker typically visible on CRT monitors.
  4. Native Digital Output: The processed signal is encoded into TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling), which is sent directly through a DVI-I or DVI-D port to ensure a clean, pixel-perfect image without analog noise.

Key Benefits

  • Zero Input Lag: Because pixel sampling occurs directly at the hardware layer via FPGA processing, video output occurs with virtually undetectable latency, making it ideal for responsive gaming.
  • Pixel-Perfect Clarity: Bypassing internal and external analog conversions prevents color bleeding, ghosting, and RF interference.
  • Configurable Resolutions: Modern internal scan doublers include software configuration tools allowing users to apply scanline emulation, adjust pixel aspect ratios, and fine-tune output frequencies to match standard modern monitor timings.
