What Is the Max Resolution of Single-Link DVI at 60 Hz?

The maximum standard resolution supported by a single-link DVI connection at a 60 Hz refresh rate is 1920 × 1200 (WUXGA) using standard CVT reduced-blanking timings, or 1920 × 1080 (Full HD 1080p) using traditional VESA blanking intervals. This limitation stems directly from the standard maximum pixel clock frequency defined in the Digital Display Working Group (DDWG) specification.

Understanding Single-Link DVI Bandwidth

Digital Visual Interface (DVI) relies on Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) to transmit digital video signals. A single-link DVI connector utilizes three TMDS data channels (one dedicated to each primary color: red, green, and blue) along with a single TMDS clock channel.

Under the original DVI 1.0 specification, a single TMDS link operates at a maximum pixel clock rate of 165 MHz. Each TMDS data channel transmits 10 bits per pixel cycle, translating to:

  • Maximum Pixel Rate: 165 million pixels per second
  • Raw Channel Throughput: 1.65 Gbps per channel
  • Total Digital Data Bandwidth: 4.95 Gbps (with a video payload throughput of roughly 3.96 Gbps)

Because the transmission bandwidth caps at 165 MHz, the maximum resolution a single-link connection can output depends on the total number of pixels sent per frame, including both visible pixels and non-visible blanking intervals.

Blanking Intervals and Supported Resolutions

Video signals require blanking intervals between lines and frames for synchronization. The timing standard used dictates which resolutions fit inside the 165 MHz bandwidth envelope:

  • 1920 × 1080 at 60 Hz (Standard Timing): Standard timing formats require roughly a 148.5 MHz pixel clock, easily fitting within the 165 MHz ceiling.
  • 1920 × 1200 at 60 Hz (Reduced Blanking): Under traditional blanking intervals, 1920 × 1200 requires a pixel clock of approximately 193 MHz, which exceeds single-link capabilities. However, VESA Coordinated Video Timings Reduced Blanking (CVT-RB) eliminates unnecessary analog-era sync overhead, reducing the required pixel clock to roughly 154 MHz. This allows 1920 × 1200 at 60 Hz to operate within the 165 MHz limit.
  • 1600 × 1200 at 60 Hz (UXGA): Standard 4:3 high-resolution displays utilize a 162 MHz pixel clock, which remains just under the single-link threshold.

Single-Link vs. Dual-Link DVI

When display requirements exceed 1920 × 1200 at 60 Hz, single-link DVI reaches its technical ceiling. Higher resolutions require dual-link DVI:

  • Dual-Link DVI Architecture: Dual-link DVI incorporates a second set of three TMDS data transmitters using the extra pins on the connector, effectively doubling the data lanes to six.
  • Dual-Link Resolutions: By bypassing the single-link bandwidth ceiling, dual-link DVI accommodates resolutions up to 2560 × 1600 (WQXGA) at 60 Hz or higher refresh rates, such as 1920 × 1080 at 120 Hz to 144 Hz on compatible monitors.

Connecting a monitor rated for resolutions higher than 1920 × 1200 to a single-link DVI source causes the display to either downscale the image, restrict the available resolution options in system settings, or lower the refresh rate to compensate for the limited bandwidth.
