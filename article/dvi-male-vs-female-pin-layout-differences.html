DVI Male vs Female Pin Layout Differences

This article explains the standard pin layout differences between DVI male and female connectors. While both connectors share the same fundamental pin-numbering system and signal definitions, they differ mechanically, topologically, and in how their pin arrays are populated. Below, you will find a direct breakdown of how the physical pin orientations, mirror-image layouts, and connector variations distinguish male DVI plugs from female DVI receptacles.

Physical Design: Pins vs. Receptacles

The most fundamental difference is the physical contact design:

  • Male Connectors (Plugs): Feature solid, protruding brass pins that project outward from the connector body. These are found on the ends of DVI cables or dongles.
  • Female Connectors (Jacks/Receptacles): Feature corresponding pinholes housing internal contact springs. These are built into devices such as graphics cards, monitors, and motherboards.

Mirrored Layout Geometry

To ensure corresponding signals make contact when plugged together, the pin layouts are mirror images of one another when viewed face-on:

  • Pin 1 Orientation: Facing a standard DVI male connector directly (with the long ground blade on the right), Pin 1 is located at the top-left of the main pin grid. Facing a standard female port directly (with the ground blade slot on the left), Pin 1 is located at the top-right.
  • Row Configuration: Both connectors organize digital data lines into three rows of eight pins (numbered 1–8 on row 1, 9–16 on row 2, and 17–24 on row 3). Because of the direct mating requirement, the columns are horizontally reversed relative to one another from a frontal view.

The Analog Ground Blade and Quadrant Pins

To the side of the digital grid sits the analog section:

  • Female Receptacles: Almost always feature a cross-shaped opening surrounded by four pin receptacles (pins C1 through C4), with the horizontal/vertical slot serving as the analog ground (C5). This cross-slot design ensures compatibility with both flat-blade DVI-D cables and cross-blade DVI-I cables.
  • Male Plugs: Vary depending on the cable type. A DVI-D male plug features only a single, flat ground blade with no surrounding C1–C4 pins. A DVI-I male plug includes the four quadrant pins alongside the blade.

Pin Population and Interoperability

Female DVI ports on source and display hardware are typically manufactured with the full 24+5 socket configuration (DVI-I Dual-Link). This allows them to physically accept any DVI male plug.

Conversely, male DVI plugs rarely populate all 29 positions unless the cable is specifically designed for dual-link analog and digital transmission. Most male connectors omit unused pins:

  • DVI-D Single Link (Male): 18 digital pins populated in two 3x3 blocks, with the center six pins omitted, plus one flat ground blade.
  • DVI-D Dual Link (Male): Full 24 digital pins populated in a solid 3x8 grid, plus one flat ground blade.
  • DVI-I Dual Link (Male): Full 24 digital pins populated, four analog pins, and one ground blade (24+5 total).
