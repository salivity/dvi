Connect Vintage PCs to CRT Monitors with DVI-I

Retro gamers frequently utilize DVI-I ports on vintage graphics cards to connect to cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors for authentic motion clarity, native resolutions, and zero input lag. Unlike modern digital-only standards, the DVI-I interface carries native analog RGB signals alongside digital data. By taking advantage of these analog pins, gamers can connect their systems to analog displays using inexpensive, passive hardware, bypassing the need for lag-inducing active signal converters.

The Role of DVI-I

DVI ports come in several variants, but the most important distinction for retro gaming is between DVI-D (Digital only) and DVI-I (Integrated). A DVI-I port integrates both digital and analog signals within a single connector. The four distinct pins surrounding the wide flat blade on a DVI-I port carry an analog RGBHV (Red, Green, Blue, Horizontal Sync, Vertical Sync) signal. This signal is electrically identical to the signal transmitted through a standard 15-pin VGA (DE-15) connector commonly found on PC CRT monitors.

Connecting via Passive Adapters

Because the analog signal is already present at the DVI-I output, no signal conversion or digital-to-analog processing is required. Gamers connect these PCs to CRT monitors using a simple, passive DVI-I to VGA adapter or a dedicated DVI-I to VGA cable. These adapters merely route the analog pins from the DVI connector to the corresponding pins on the VGA input of the CRT monitor. Because the process is entirely passive, it introduces zero milliseconds of input latency and prevents the signal degradation often caused by budget digital-to-analog converters (DACs).

Compatible Hardware

This connection method relies on graphics cards released during the transitional era of PC gaming—roughly from the early 2000s up through the mid-2010s. Popular retro gaming GPUs, such as NVIDIA’s GeForce FX, 6000, 7000, 8000, and GTX series up to the GTX 900 series, frequently featured at least one DVI-I port. Similarly, ATI/AMD Radeon cards up through the R9 300 series widely included DVI-I outputs. Modern graphics cards (such as NVIDIA GeForce 10-series and newer, or AMD RX-series) completely phased out DVI-I in favor of DVI-D, HDMI, and DisplayPort, making older cards uniquely valuable for direct CRT connectivity.

Configuration and Resolution Support

Once connected, retro gamers use operating system display settings or software utilities like Custom Resolution Utility (CRU) and graphics control panels to drive the CRT. CRTs lack a fixed pixel grid, allowing them to display multiple resolutions natively without scaling artifacts. Through a DVI-I to VGA connection, vintage PCs can output standard retro PC resolutions such as 640x480, 800x600, or 1024x768 at high refresh rates (often 75Hz to 120Hz or higher), as well as low-resolution 240p signals for emulating classic home consoles with authentic scanlines.
