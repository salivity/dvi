Native Aspect Ratio for Dual-Link DVI 2560x1600

The native aspect ratio supported by a dual-link DVI connection running at a resolution of 2560x1600 is 16:10. This article covers the mathematics behind this resolution, explains why dual-link DVI was necessary to achieve it, and highlights the historical role this display standard played in professional computing environments.

The 16:10 Aspect Ratio

The resolution of 2560 pixels horizontally by 1600 pixels vertically calculates to a simplified fraction of 16:10 (or 8:5). Dividing 2560 by 160 yields 16, and dividing 1600 by 160 yields 10.

While consumer multimedia displays eventually shifted predominantly toward 16:9 (such as 1920x1080 or 2560x1440), the 16:10 format was the preferred standard for high-end computer monitors and workstations. The extra vertical space provided by 1600 pixels offered significant utility for productivity, coding, image editing, and viewing full two-page document layouts side by side.

Why Dual-Link DVI Was Required

Single-link DVI connections utilize a single TMDS (Transition Minimized Differential Signaling) transmitter, limiting the interface to a pixel clock of 165 MHz. This restricted single-link DVI to a maximum resolution of 1920x1200 at 60Hz.

To support higher resolutions like 2560x1600:

  • Doubled Bandwidth: Dual-link DVI integrates a second TMDS transmitter, utilizing six data channels instead of three.
  • Increased Pixel Clock: This hardware upgrade allows for a pixel clock exceeding 300 MHz, providing sufficient throughput for the nearly 4.1 million pixels rendered at 2560x1600 at a standard 60Hz refresh rate.
  • Physical Pinout: Dual-link DVI cables feature a full 24-pin grid, whereas single-link connectors omit the center six pins.

Common Applications

The 2560x1600 resolution over dual-link DVI was the hallmark of premium 30-inch computer monitors produced from the mid-2000s to the early 2010s. Notable examples include the Apple Cinema HD Display 30-inch, Dell UltraSharp U3011, and HP LP3065. These displays relied entirely on dual-link DVI to deliver high-resolution computing before modern standards like DisplayPort and HDMI 2.0 became universal.
