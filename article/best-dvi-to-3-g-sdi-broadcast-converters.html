Best DVI to 3G-SDI Broadcast Converters

Integrating computer graphics, presentation laptops, and legacy digital displays into a professional television or live event workflow requires specialized hardware conversion. Broadcast converter boxes bridge this gap by converting computer-standard DVI signals into SMPTE-compliant 3G-SDI feeds. This guide outlines the top broadcast-grade converter boxes capable of transforming DVI computer outputs to 3G-SDI, highlighting their scaling capabilities, audio integration, and technical reliability.

AJA ROI-DVI

The AJA ROI-DVI is specifically engineered for scanning and converting computer DVI outputs into broadcast SDI. Rather than merely changing the physical interface, it includes real-time hardware scaling, aspect ratio management, and region-of-interest selection.

  • Input: DVI-D female (supports non-standard VESA computer resolutions) and 3.5mm analog audio.
  • Output: 3G/HD/SD-SDI (SMPTE 259/292/424) and loop-through DVI.
  • Key Features: Full 10-bit processing, reference/genlock input, audio embedding, and USB configuration via AJA Mini-Config software.

Datavideo DAC-70

The Datavideo DAC-70 is an up/down/cross converter widely utilized in broadcast environments for its versatility with diverse input signals, including DVI, HDMI, and VGA.

  • Input: DVI-D, VGA, HDMI, and stereo RCA audio.
  • Output: Dual 3G/HD/SD-SDI outputs and one HDMI output.
  • Key Features: Built-in frame synchronizer, automatic detection of input formats, hardware up/down conversion to standard television resolutions (1080p, 1080i, 720p), and external dip switches for quick setup without a computer.

Roland VC-1-SC

The Roland VC-1-SC is an all-in-one scan converter designed for live production and broadcast applications. It accepts RGB/DVI, HDMI, and composite video, scaling them accurately to 3G-SDI formats.

  • Input: DVI-I (accepts both digital DVI-D and analog DVI-A/VGA), HDMI, composite, and analog audio.
  • Output: 3G/HD/SD-SDI and HDMI.
  • Key Features: Internal frame synchronizer, genlock capability, audio delay management to resolve lip-sync issues, and test pattern generator.

Decimator MD-HX (with DVI-to-HDMI Adapter)

While technically equipped with an HDMI port, the Decimator MD-HX frequently serves as the broadcast industry standard for DVI conversion via a passive DVI-to-HDMI cable. Because DVI-D and HDMI share the same underlying digital video signaling, the MD-HX handles the conversion seamlessly.

  • Input: HDMI/DVI-D and 3G/HD/SD-SDI.
  • Output: 3G/HD/SD-SDI (multiple assignable ports) and HDMI.
  • Key Features: Robust cross-converter, built-in LCD screen and button controls, full 14-mode cross-conversion matrix, and high tolerance for non-standard computer refresh rates.

Kramer VP-483

The Kramer VP-483 is a dedicated broadcast-grade format converter designed to translate DVI signals directly into 3G-SDI.

  • Input: DVI-I connector (configured for DVI-D) and unbalanced stereo audio.
  • Output: Dual 3G-SDI outputs.
  • Key Features: Multi-standard operation (SMPTE 424M, 292M, 259M), active input loop-through, and automatic audio embedding into the SDI output stream.

Key Considerations When Converting DVI to 3G-SDI

When choosing a converter box for DVI-to-SDI conversion, prioritize the following technical factors:

  • Resolution and Refresh Rates: Computers typically output VESA resolutions at variable refresh rates (e.g., 1920x1200 at 60Hz), whereas broadcast systems strictly require SMPTE formats (e.g., 1920x1080 at 59.94Hz). Units with built-in scalers and frame synchronizers (like the AJA ROI or Datavideo DAC-70) are mandatory if the computer cannot be locked to broadcast timings.
  • Audio Embedding: DVI-D carries video only. If the output needs audio embedded into the SDI stream, select a converter equipped with a 3.5mm or RCA analog audio input.
  • EDID Management: A converter box with onboard EDID emulation ensures the host computer outputs a stable, compatible resolution without defaulting to an unsupported display mode.
