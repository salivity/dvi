How TMDS Encoding Reduces EMI in DVI Cables

Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) is the core signaling technology used in DVI and HDMI cables to transfer high-bandwidth digital video while keeping electromagnetic interference (EMI) to a minimum. By using an 8b/10b encoding algorithm that minimizes signal transitions, maintains direct current (DC) balance, and transmits data over complementary differential pairs, TMDS prevents the cable from acting as an efficient radiating antenna. This architecture enables high-speed data transmission across copper cables without emitting excessive electromagnetic radiation that could disrupt nearby electronic devices.

High-frequency electromagnetic radiation in digital cables is primarily caused by rapid state changes—switching between logical high and low voltages. The steeper and more frequent these voltage transitions are, the more high-frequency energy is radiated into the surrounding environment. TMDS mitigates this phenomenon through three foundational mechanisms:

Transition Minimization

In the first stage of TMDS encoding, an 8-bit data byte is converted into a 10-bit code word. The encoder evaluates the incoming byte and determines whether encoding it with XOR or XNOR logic will result in fewer transitions between logical zeros and ones. A dedicated ninth bit is added to indicate which logic operation was applied so the receiver can reconstruct the original byte. By actively selecting the path that results in the fewest voltage swings, the frequency of edge transitions is substantially lowered, cutting down high-frequency harmonic generation and the resulting EMI.

DC Balancing

Signal lines that experience a disproportionate number of ones or zeros over time suffer from DC drift, which generates common-mode current—a primary driver of radiated emissions. TMDS solves this by evaluating the running disparity, which tracks the cumulative difference between transmitted zeros and ones. If too many ones have been sent, the encoder sets a tenth bit to invert the encoded data, pulling the average signal level back toward neutral. Maintaining this DC balance ensures a stable baseline voltage across the transmission medium, eliminating common-mode current buildup.

Differential Signaling

Once the 10-bit symbol is generated, TMDS transmits the signal across a shielded twisted-pair wire using differential signaling. Instead of referencing a single wire to ground, the signal is sent simultaneously over two conductors: one carries the true signal, while the other carries an exact inverted counterpart. Because the currents flow in equal and opposite directions in tightly twisted wires, their respective magnetic fields overlap and cancel each other out. This field cancellation prevents radiation from escaping the cable and simultaneously renders the transmission immune to external electromagnetic noise.
