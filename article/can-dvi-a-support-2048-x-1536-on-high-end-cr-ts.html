Can DVI-A Support 2048x1536 on High-End CRTs?

DVI-A is fully capable of carrying analog resolutions up to 2048x1536 and beyond to high-end CRT monitors. Because DVI-A transmits the exact same analog RGBHV signals as a traditional VGA interface, it is not bound by digital transmission limits like Single-Link DVI's 165 MHz pixel clock. Instead, performance depends strictly on the graphics card's digital-to-analog converter (RAMDAC), the quality of the cabling, and the CRT’s maximum horizontal scan rate.

The Mechanics of DVI-A

DVI-A (DVI-Analog) utilizes the analog pins located around the flat blade on the DVI connector (pins C1 through C4, along with C5 and the dedicated sync lines). This configuration bypasses the digital TMDS lines entirely and directly outputs raw red, green, blue, horizontal sync, and vertical sync signals. As a result, a DVI-A output functions essentially as a standard 15-pin VGA or 5-BNC connection packaged in a DVI form factor.

RAMDAC and Pixel Clock Demands

Because analog video signals lack strict resolution metadata caps, the maximum resolution is governed by the graphics card’s internal RAMDAC frequency:

  • 2048x1536 at 60 Hz requires a pixel clock of approximately 268 MHz (using standard VESA CVT timings).
  • 2048x1536 at 85 Hz pushes the pixel clock to roughly 388 MHz.

Most late-generation graphics cards equipped with native DVI-I/DVI-A ports (such as the Nvidia GeForce 8000/9000 series or ATI Radeon HD series) feature integrated RAMDACs operating at 400 MHz. This provides more than enough bandwidth to generate a stable, full-color 2048x1536 analog image.

CRT Monitor Capabilities

Displaying 2048x1536 requires a monitor with an exceptionally high horizontal deflection rate (horizontal scan frequency, measured in kHz):

  • Running 2048x1536 at 60 Hz requires a CRT capable of at least a 96 kHz to 100 kHz horizontal scan rate.
  • Running 2048x1536 at an ergonomic 85 Hz requires a top-tier CRT capable of 130 kHz to 140 kHz.

Flagship 21-inch and 22-inch CRT displays—such as the Sony CPD-G520, ViewSonic P225f, Iiyama Vision Master Pro 514, or the widescreen Sony GDM-FW900 (using proportional resolutions)—regularly handled these parameters natively over analog inputs.

Signal Integrity and Cables

At a pixel clock approaching 400 MHz, high-frequency analog signals are highly susceptible to interference and attenuation. Achieving a sharp 2048x1536 image requires high-grade, individually shielded coaxial cabling (typically a DVI-to-VGA or DVI-to-5x-BNC breakout cable). Substandard cables at this resolution often introduce ringing, ghosting, or high-frequency blur across the screen. When paired with high-quality components, DVI-A provides the full analog throughput necessary to drive high-end CRTs at 2048x1536.
