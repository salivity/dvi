DVI-I to Analog Projector Passive Breakout Cable

A DVI-I output can successfully drive an analog projector using a passive breakout cable because the DVI-I standard natively carries both digital and analog video signals. Because the analog signal is already present at the port, no active digital-to-analog converter (DAC) is required, allowing an inexpensive passive cable or adapter to route the signal directly to an analog input like VGA or RGBHV.

How DVI-I Delivers Analog Signals

The "I" in DVI-I stands for "Integrated." Unlike DVI-D, which exclusively transmits digital data, DVI-I includes dedicated physical pins for analog RGB (Red, Green, Blue) and horizontal/vertical synchronization signals.

These analog signals are located on the cluster of four pins surrounding the wide, flat ground blade on the side of the DVI connector:

  • Pin C1: Red analog video
  • Pin C2: Green analog video
  • Pin C3: Blue analog video
  • Pin C4: Horizontal sync
  • Pin C5: Analog ground (the flat blade)

Because these analog pins output the exact electrical signal required by standard VGA and analog RGB displays, a passive breakout cable only needs to map these pins to the corresponding pins on the projector's analog connector (such as a 15-pin VGA connector or multiple BNC jacks).

Requirements for Passive Operation

To ensure the connection functions correctly, verify the following:

  1. Verify the Source Port is DVI-I: Inspect the female port on your source device (such as a graphics card). It must have the cross-shaped grid with four pin holes around the flat blade slot. If the port only features a single flat horizontal slot with no surrounding pin holes, it is a digital-only DVI-D port and will not work with a passive cable.
  2. Projector Input Compatibility: Most passive breakout cables route DVI-I to a standard VGA (HD-15) connector or component/RGBHV BNC connectors. Ensure the projector's selected input matches the breakout cable configuration.
  3. Resolution and Refresh Rate Matching: Analog inputs do not always support high refresh rates or modern non-standard wide resolutions. Ensure the source device outputs a display resolution and refresh rate supported by the analog projector's specifications.
