Codecs Used by DVI-over-IP Hardware Encoders

DVI-over-IP hardware encoders convert digital visual interface (DVI) video signals into data packets for transmission across standard Ethernet networks. To balance visual fidelity, network bandwidth, and transmission latency, these devices utilize various video compression standards ranging from uncompressed stream processing to highly compressed inter-frame algorithms. This article examines the most common codecs implemented in DVI-over-IP systems, detailing their technical performance characteristics and practical deployment environments.

H.264 (MPEG-4 AVC)

H.264 is among the most widely supported codecs in legacy and standard DVI-over-IP deployments. It utilizes discrete cosine transform (DCT) and inter-frame predictive compression, allowing high-definition DVI video to stream over low-bandwidth infrastructure, typically between 5 Mbps and 30 Mbps per stream.

  • Primary Use Case: Enterprise digital signage, educational streaming, and campus-wide AV distribution where network bandwidth is limited or transmission over WANs is necessary.
  • Latency Profile: Moderate to high (typically 50 ms to 250 ms), making it less ideal for high-precision, real-time mouse interaction in KVM environments.

H.265 (HEVC)

High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC) provides roughly 50% better data compression efficiency compared to H.264 at equivalent quality levels. In modern DVI-over-IP hardware, H.265 is often selected when high-resolution, high-frame-rate DVI sources must be routed over standard 1 Gbps or sub-gigabit corporate networks.

  • Primary Use Case: Bandwidth-constrained deployments requiring higher fidelity or simultaneous distribution of multiple high-definition channels.
  • Latency Profile: Similar to or slightly higher than H.264 due to the computational complexity required for encoding and decoding.

JPEG 2000

JPEG 2000 is an intra-frame, wavelet-based compression standard extensively used in professional AV-over-IP ecosystems designed for 1 Gbps networks. Because it compresses each frame independently without temporal prediction, it avoids the motion artifacts common to inter-frame codecs.

  • Primary Use Case: Mission-critical control rooms, broadcast monitoring, and medical environments where frame integrity, low latency, and artifact-free static imagery (such as text, schematics, and UI elements) are paramount.
  • Latency Profile: Low latency (typically 1 to 2 frames, approximately 16 ms to 35 ms).
  • Bandwidth Requirements: High-efficiency profiles typically consume 100 Mbps to 800 Mbps per stream.

JPEG-XS

JPEG-XS is an ultra-low-latency intra-frame codec designed specifically for production networks and high-end AV installations operating over 1 Gbps and 10 Gbps networks. It provides visually lossless compression at low compression ratios (typically between 2:1 and 10:1).

  • Primary Use Case: Real-time interactive systems, high-end KVM extension, and live event distribution where near-zero lag is mandatory.
  • Latency Profile: Sub-millisecond (end-to-end latency measured in microsecond or fractional-frame scales).

Uncompressed and SDVoE (AptoVision BlueRiver)

Certain enterprise DVI-over-IP encoders bypass traditional lossy compression entirely or utilize minimal pixel-pipeline compression over 10 Gbps Ethernet infrastructure, such as platforms standardized under Software Defined Video Over Ethernet (SDVoE).

  • Primary Use Case: Medical imaging, flight simulation, and zero-latency command centers where exact pixel replication and real-time interaction are required.
  • Latency Profile: True zero-frame latency (under 100 microseconds).
  • Bandwidth Requirements: Requires dedicated 10 Gbps network switches and cabling.

Proprietary Lightweight Codecs

Several ProAV manufacturers implement proprietary intra-frame or run-length encoding algorithms within specialized FPGAs or ASICs. These custom codecs focus on computer-generated graphics common to DVI interfaces, optimizing the compression of solid color blocks, fonts, and desktop GUIs rather than continuous motion video.

  • Primary Use Case: Specialized KVM extenders and industrial workstation management.
  • Latency Profile: Sub-frame latency (typically 5 ms to 15 ms) with minimal CPU and network overhead.
