What Is Intra-Pair Skew in DVI Cable Construction?

Intra-pair skew in DVI cable construction refers to the arrival time difference between the two complementary signals traveling within a single differential twisted pair. In Digital Visual Interface (DVI) cables, video data is transmitted using Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS), which relies on precise timing between paired positive and negative conductors. When physical or electrical imbalances cause one signal to arrive faster than the other, the resulting intra-pair skew degrades signal integrity, potentially causing pixel errors, screen flickering, or total loss of the video display.

The Role of Differential Signaling in DVI

DVI uses TMDS to send high-speed digital video data across multiple shielded twisted pairs. Each data channel—red, green, blue, and the clock—consists of two wires: a positive line and a negative line carrying identical signals in opposite phase. At the receiving end, the display calculates the voltage difference between these two lines to reconstruct the digital data stream. This technique naturally cancels out external electromagnetic interference (EMI) and common-mode noise, provided both signals remain precisely synchronized.

Defining Intra-Pair Skew

Intra-pair skew is the time delay difference (measured in picoseconds) between the two conductors within the same twisted pair. It is distinct from inter-pair skew, which is the timing difference between different pairs (such as between the red channel and the clock channel).

Because differential receivers rely on both phases of the signal transitioning at the exact same instant, any time displacement causes the reconstructed waveform to become distorted.

Causes of Intra-Pair Skew in Cable Construction

During the manufacturing of a DVI cable, intra-pair skew is typically introduced through physical and material inconsistencies:

  • Conductor Length Mismatches: If one wire inside the pair is cut or twisted slightly longer than the other, the signal on the longer wire takes more time to reach the display.
  • Dielectric Inconsistencies: The insulation around the copper conductors determines the speed at which electrical signals propagate. Variations in the dielectric constant, thickness, or foam density of the insulation alter the velocity of propagation between the two wires.
  • Twisting Variations (Lay Length): Uneven mechanical tension during the twisting process can alter the physical geometry of the pair, leading to variations in mutual capacitance and inductance.
  • Bending and Mechanical Stress: When a cable is bent or subjected to tight assembly bends, the outer conductor of the pair stretches more than the inner conductor, inducing delay mismatch.

Effects on Video Performance

As intra-pair skew increases, the differential signal deteriorates:

  1. Eye Diagram Closure: High skew closes the "eye" of the signal in an oscilloscope eye-diagram, shrinking the timing window in which the receiver can reliably sample data bits.
  2. Increased Common-Mode Noise: When the two opposing signals are misaligned, they no longer perfectly cancel each other out, generating electromagnetic emissions and making the cable more susceptible to external interference.
  3. Display Artifacts: Mild skew results in "sparkles," pixel noise, or color dropouts. Severe intra-pair skew leads to periodic signal dropouts or an unsupported resolution error on the monitor.

To meet DVI specifications and support high-resolution formats (such as 1080p or 2560×1600 on dual-link connections), cable manufacturers must strictly control twisting consistency, conductor tolerances, and insulation materials to keep intra-pair skew within a tolerance of mere picoseconds.
