Difference Between CL2 and CL3 Rated DVI Cables

When running DVI cables through walls, selecting the correct fire-safety rating is critical for building code compliance and personal safety. The fundamental difference between CL2 and CL3 rated DVI cables lies in their voltage-carrying capacity: CL2 is rated for up to 150 volts, whereas CL3 is rated for up to 300 volts. Both ratings share identical flame-retardant properties to prevent the spread of fire along the cable jacket inside wall cavities, but CL3 offers higher surge protection and broader commercial applicability.

Understanding CL Ratings

CL stands for "Class" and is defined by the National Electrical Code (NEC). These ratings designate low-voltage cables suitable for in-wall residential and commercial installations. Unrated cables emit toxic fumes and spread fire quickly if ignited inside enclosed wall spaces. CL2 and CL3 jackets are engineered to self-extinguish and resist burning, ensuring that a fire does not easily travel through internal building infrastructure.

Voltage Capacity

The primary technical distinction between the two classes is voltage tolerance:

  • CL2 (Class 2): Rated to handle electrical surges and operations up to 150 volts.
  • CL3 (Class 3): Rated to handle electrical surges and operations up to 300 volts.

Because DVI (Digital Visual Interface) cables typically transmit low-voltage video signals (usually around 3.3 to 5 volts), the native signal voltage of DVI is well below the threshold of both ratings. However, the higher 300-volt threshold of CL3 allows the cable to withstand significantly higher accidental electrical surges or interference from adjacent wiring.

Flame Resistance and In-Wall Safety

From a fire-safety standpoint, CL2 and CL3 share the same baseline flammability test standards (UL 13). Both types of jackets are treated with flame-retardant compounds to prevent flames from traveling vertically or horizontally through walls. Neither cable rating is certified for use in drop ceilings or ventilation spaces; those installations require a CL2P or CL3P "plenum" rating.

Interchangability and Substitution

Under NEC guidelines, a higher-rated cable can always substitute for a lower-rated cable, but not the other way around:

  • CL3 can replace CL2: You can safely use a CL3 rated DVI cable in any application that calls for a CL2 cable.
  • CL2 cannot replace CL3: If a commercial building code or project blueprint specifically demands CL3, a CL2 cable cannot be used.

Which Cable Should You Choose?

For typical residential in-wall DVI setups—such as connecting a media PC to a wall-mounted display—a CL2 rated DVI cable provides more than enough protection and fully satisfies standard residential building codes.

If you are installing the cable in a commercial setting, running the cable alongside high-voltage electrical lines, or want extra protection against unexpected power surges, choose a CL3 rated DVI cable.
