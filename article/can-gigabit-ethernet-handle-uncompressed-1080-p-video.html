Can Gigabit Ethernet Handle Uncompressed 1080p Video?

A standard Gigabit Ethernet network cannot handle an uncompressed 1080p DVI video stream. Transmitting raw, uncompressed 1080p video at standard frame rates requires bandwidth that significantly exceeds the maximum capacity of a 1 Gbps connection. To distribute full HD video across a gigabit infrastructure, you must either compress the video signal or upgrade your physical network to a higher standard, such as 10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE).

The bandwidth limitation becomes obvious when calculating the raw data rate of standard DVI video. A 1080p display consists of 1920 horizontal pixels and 1080 vertical pixels, resulting in roughly 2.07 million pixels per frame. DVI typically uses 24-bit TrueColor (8 bits per channel for red, green, and blue). Multiplying the pixel count by 24 bits yields approximately 49.77 megabits per frame. At a standard refresh rate of 60 frames per second (fps), the baseline active video data rate is roughly 2.98 gigabits per second (Gbps). When accounting for DVI blanking intervals and timing overhead, the required transmission rate often surpasses 3 Gbps.

Gigabit Ethernet (1000BASE-T) has a theoretical maximum throughput of 1,000 Mbps (1 Gbps). After factoring in protocol overhead from Ethernet frames, IP packets, and transmission control protocols (TCP or UDP), the real-world usable payload bandwidth drops to around 940 to 950 Mbps. Comparing the required ~3 Gbps stream to the available ~0.94 Gbps throughput shows an immediate deficit: uncompressed 1080p60 demands more than triple the total capacity of a gigabit link. Even lowering the frame rate to 30 fps (~1.49 Gbps) or cinematic 24 fps (~1.19 Gbps) still exceeds the physical limit of a gigabit connection.

To successfully transmit 1080p video over a Gigabit Ethernet network, compression is mandatory. Technologies such as H.264, H.265 (HEVC), and AV1 can compress 1080p video down to bitrates between 5 Mbps and 30 Mbps with minimal perceptual quality loss, easily fitting within gigabit limits. For broadcast or production environments prioritizing ultra-low latency, mezzanine codecs like NDI or JPEG-XS compress high-definition video into the 100 to 200 Mbps range. Alternatively, if truly uncompressed video delivery is required, the underlying network infrastructure must be upgraded to 10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE) or dedicated video extension standards like HDBaseT.
