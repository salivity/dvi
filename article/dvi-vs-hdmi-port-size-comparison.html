DVI vs HDMI Port Size Comparison

This article examines the physical size differences between a standard Digital Visual Interface (DVI) receptacle and a standard High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI Type-A) port. While both standards transmit uncompressed digital video signals, their physical form factors reflect different eras of hardware design, with HDMI offering a significantly more compact profile compared to the bulky, hardware-secured footprint of DVI.

Standard DVI Receptacle Dimensions

A standard full-size DVI female receptacle (applicable to DVI-D, DVI-I, and DVI-A) is one of the largest consumer display connectors ever standardized.

  • Connector Body (Shield): The metal-shielded opening measures approximately 26.1 mm in width and 9.5 mm in height.
  • Full Receptacle Footprint (Including Hardware): Because DVI relies on threaded hex standoffs to accept thumbscrews for physical retention, the actual chassis cutout required on a computer or monitor is roughly 39.5 mm wide by 15.5 mm high.
  • Internal Depth: The female receptacle typically extends roughly 15 mm to 20 mm deep into the device’s internal chassis, occupying significant circuit board area.

Standard HDMI (Type-A) Port Dimensions

The standard HDMI Type-A connector was engineered specifically to reduce connector footprint and eliminate the need for manual screw-fastening mechanisms.

  • Receptacle Opening: A standard female HDMI Type-A port measures approximately 13.9 mm to 14.0 mm in width and 4.45 mm to 4.55 mm in height.
  • Full Receptacle Footprint: Because HDMI relies solely on a friction-fit retention system with internal spring-latch contacts, it requires no external mounting posts. The total faceplate area consumed by an HDMI port is roughly 15 mm wide by 6 mm high.
  • Internal Depth: A standard surface-mount or through-hole HDMI port extends roughly 10 mm to 11.5 mm onto the printed circuit board.

Direct Dimensional Comparison

Comparing the two standards reveals the substantial space savings offered by HDMI:

  • Width: At approximately 14 mm, an HDMI port is roughly half the width of the core DVI connector shell (26.1 mm) and barely more than one-third the width of a DVI port with its screw standoffs included (39.5 mm).
  • Height: At approximately 4.5 mm, an HDMI port is less than half the height of the DVI shield (9.5 mm) and less than a third of the total height of a DVI port assembly (15.5 mm).
  • Faceplate Surface Area: A complete DVI connector footprint occupies over 600 mm² of back-panel real estate, whereas an HDMI Type-A port occupies roughly 90 mm².

In practical terms, the space required to install a single DVI receptacle on a graphics card or display panel can easily accommodate three to four full-sized HDMI ports. This drastic size reduction is the primary physical reason modern ultra-thin laptops, compact graphics cards, and sleek monitors abandoned DVI in favor of HDMI and DisplayPort.
