Unused Pins in Single-Link DVI Explained

Digital Visual Interface (DVI) cables and ports often appear with missing physical pins or unconnected terminals depending on whether they support single-link or dual-link operation. Specifically, pins 4, 5, 12, 13, 20, and 21 are left unused or entirely omitted in single-link DVI connectors because they are dedicated to a second data transmission link that single-link operation does not utilize. This article breaks down the technical reasons behind this pin arrangement, how Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) dictates pin assignments, and why these pins are reserved exclusively for dual-link configurations.

Understanding DVI and TMDS Architecture

DVI transmits digital video using TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling). TMDS uses differential pairs of wires to carry color data (Red, Green, and Blue) along with a dedicated clock signal to synchronize the data stream.

A single TMDS link consists of:

  • Three differential data channels (carrying RGB signals)
  • One differential clock channel

Each differential channel requires two physical wires (a positive signal and an inverted negative signal) to cancel out electromagnetic interference, meaning each TMDS channel consumes two pins on the connector.

The Role of the Unused Pins

A single-link DVI connection supplies enough bandwidth to drive displays up to a resolution of 1920×1200 at 60 Hz (165 MHz pixel clock). It achieves this using TMDS Data Channels 0, 1, and 2, which correspond to the following pins:

  • TMDS Data 2 (+/-): Pins 2 and 1
  • TMDS Data 1 (+/-): Pins 10 and 9
  • TMDS Data 0 (+/-): Pins 18 and 17
  • TMDS Clock (+/-): Pins 23 and 24

When higher resolutions or refresh rates are required (such as 2560×1600 at 60 Hz or 1080p at 144 Hz), a second link—creating a "dual-link" connection—must be engaged. This second link adds three more differential data channels: TMDS Data Channels 3, 4, and 5 (sharing the original clock).

The pins designated for this secondary link are:

  • Pin 4: TMDS Data 4-
  • Pin 5: TMDS Data 4+
  • Pin 12: TMDS Data 3-
  • Pin 13: TMDS Data 3+
  • Pin 20: TMDS Data 5-
  • Pin 21: TMDS Data 5+

Why Single-Link Leaves These Pins Disconnected

Because single-link devices lack the transmitter hardware to output signals on TMDS channels 3, 4, and 5, pins 4, 5, 12, 13, 20, and 21 serve no functional purpose in a single-link environment.

In single-link cables, manufacturers either leave these pin positions completely empty (creating a visible gap between two blocks of nine pins) or leave the physical pins disconnected internally. Doing so provides multiple practical advantages:

  1. Lower Manufacturing Cost: Omitting the internal twisted pairs of copper wiring reduces material costs and assembly complexity.
  2. Cable Flexibility: Fewer internal shielded pairs make the cable thinner, lighter, and easier to bend.
  3. Reduced Signal Interference: Leaving inactive pins unpopulated prevents them from acting as open-ended antennas that could pick up or radiate electromagnetic noise.

In summary, pins 4, 5, 12, 13, 20, and 21 form the differential pairs for TMDS Data Channels 3, 4, and 5. In single-link DVI, these channels do not exist, making the pins redundant and unused.
