In-Wall DVI Cable Fire Ratings and Codes

Installing DVI cables inside walls requires compliance with building codes and fire-safety standards to prevent toxic smoke spread and rapid flame travel during a fire. This guide details the essential fire-rating certifications defined by the National Electrical Code (NEC) and testing bodies like Underwriters Laboratories (UL), explaining when you need CL2, CL3, CMR (Riser), or CMP (Plenum) rated DVI cables for both residential and commercial applications.

Why Fire Ratings Matter for In-Wall Cables

Standard AV cables often have outer jackets made of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) that release toxic, corrosive fumes and burn quickly when exposed to flame. Running non-rated cables through hidden wall cavities, ceilings, or HVAC paths creates a fire hazard and violates local building codes, which can void property insurance or cause failed building inspections.

In-wall DVI cables must be certified by a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory (NRTL), such as UL or ETL, and physically stamped with their fire rating on the jacket.

Key In-Wall Fire-Rating Certifications

The National Electrical Code (NEC) outlines four primary fire ratings relevant to low-voltage audiovisual cabling like DVI:

1. CL2 (Class 2 Remote-Control, Signaling, and Power-Limited Cable)

  • Best For: Standard residential in-wall installations.
  • Details: CL2 cables are designed for general in-wall use within a single floor of a residential home. The jacket is treated to self-extinguish and prevent flames from spreading up the interior of a drywall cavity. It safely supports voltage surges up to 150 volts.

2. CL3 (Class 3 Cable)

  • Best For: Commercial standard walls and higher-voltage residential setups.
  • Details: CL3 cables undergo the same flame-spread testing as CL2 but are rated for higher voltages (up to 300 volts). A CL3 cable can always be used in place of a CL2 cable.

3. CMR (Communications Riser)

  • Best For: Vertical runs between multiple floors in non-plenum spaces.
  • Details: Tested under UL 1666, CMR-rated cables prevent fires from traveling vertically from floor to floor via elevator shafts, pipe runs, or electrical chases. If your DVI cable travels between stories within a wall, CMR (or CL2R/CL3R) is legally required.

4. CMP (Communications Plenum)

  • Best For: Drop ceilings, raised floors, and any air-handling spaces.
  • Details: Plenum spaces are areas used for environmental air circulation (such as HVAC return-air paths above acoustic ceiling tiles). Because fires in these areas can quickly circulate lethal smoke through an entire building, CMP-rated cables are subject to the strictest fire-test standard (NFPA 262 / UL 910). They use special fluoropolymer coatings that emit minimal smoke and possess high flame resistance. CMP can replace CMR, CL3, and CL2.

How to Choose the Right DVI Cable

  1. Inside a standard residential wall: Look for a cable stamped CL2 or CL3.
  2. Inside commercial walls (no air-return): Use CL3 or CMR.
  3. Between floors: Use CMR (Riser) or CMP (Plenum).
  4. Above drop ceilings used for air handling: Use CMP (Plenum) exclusively.
  5. Alternative approach: If finding a certified in-wall DVI cable of the required length is difficult, run standard DVI or fiber-optic DVI through properly rated flexible non-metallic conduit (smurf tube) rated for in-wall or plenum use.
