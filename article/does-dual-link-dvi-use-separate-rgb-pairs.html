Does Dual-Link DVI Use Separate RGB Pairs?

This article examines the internal signaling architecture of dual-link Digital Visual Interface (DVI) connections, specifically answering whether the second link incorporates separate red, green, and blue data pairs. It details the physical pin layout, the role of Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) channels, and how bandwidth is distributed between the primary and secondary links to support high-resolution displays.

Yes, dual-link DVI uses a completely separate set of physical differential pairs for red, green, and blue color data to form the second link.

In a standard single-link DVI implementation, video data travels across four differential pairs using Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS). Three of these pairs are dedicated to color components: Data 0 carries Blue along with horizontal and vertical sync signals, Data 1 carries Green, and Data 2 carries Red. The fourth pair transmits the TMDS clock, which synchronizes the data streams. A single link is limited to a maximum pixel clock of 165 MHz, sufficient for displays up to 1920×1200 at 60 Hz.

Dual-link DVI doubles the transmission bandwidth by populating the center pins of the 24-pin DVI connector (pins 4, 5, 12, 13, 19, and 20). These six pins form three additional differential data pairs:

  • TMDS Data 3: Second Blue pair (pins 4 and 5)
  • TMDS Data 4: Second Green pair (pins 12 and 13)
  • TMDS Data 5: Second Red pair (pins 19 and 20)

While the red, green, and blue channels are duplicated to create the second link, the TMDS clock signal is not. Both the primary link (Data 0, 1, and 2) and the secondary link (Data 3, 4, and 5) share the single original TMDS clock pair (pins 23 and 24).

When operating in dual-link mode, the interface alternates pixel transmission across the two sets of RGB lines. The primary link carries odd-numbered pixels, while the secondary link simultaneously carries even-numbered pixels across its own dedicated RGB pairs. By sending two complete pixels per clock cycle, dual-link DVI doubles the maximum throughput to support resolutions such as 2560×1600 at 60 Hz or 1920×1080 at 144 Hz.
