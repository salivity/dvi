DVI Cable TMDS Shielding Requirements

Certified DVI (Digital Visual Interface) cables rely on strict physical construction standards to maintain signal integrity and prevent electromagnetic interference across high-speed data channels. This article outlines the specific shielding mandated for Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) lines inside a certified DVI cable according to the Digital Display Working Group (DDWG) specification, covering both pair-level and cable-level shielding requirements.

Inside a certified DVI cable, TMDS lines are mandated to be constructed as individually shielded twisted pairs (STP). Each differential signal pair—consisting of the positive and negative data lines, as well as the TMDS clock lines—must be enclosed within its own dedicated shield. This pair-level shield typically consists of an aluminum/polyester (Mylar) foil wrap with 100% coverage, paired with a dedicated tinned copper drain wire. This individual isolation is required to prevent intra-cable crosstalk between adjacent high-speed differential pairs and to maintain a continuous, controlled differential impedance of 100 ohms (±10%).

Beyond individual line protection, the entire cable assembly requires an overall multi-layer shield. The certified standard mandates an overall outer shielding system surrounding all internal components, which typically comprises an aluminum foil layer backed by a high-coverage tinned copper braid (generally 85% coverage or higher).

To ensure proper electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) and noise dissipation, the individual TMDS drain wires connect to the designated TMDS shield ground pins on the DVI connector pinout, while the overall outer braided shield terminates to the metal connector shell. This comprehensive two-tier shielding architecture ensures the cable passes rigorous FCC and CE emissions tests while preventing high-frequency signal degradation over specified cable lengths.
