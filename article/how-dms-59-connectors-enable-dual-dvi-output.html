How DMS-59 Connectors Enable Dual DVI Output

The DMS-59 (Dual Monitor Solution, 59 pins) connector was an interface engineered to deliver dual-display capability from a single graphics card slot. By consolidating the pin arrangements of two separate video outputs into one compact receptacle, graphics card manufacturers allowed small-form-factor and low-profile systems to drive two independent monitors using a breakout cable. This article covers how DMS-59 was physically structured, how it routed video signals to support two distinct DVI channels, and how it operated without requiring active signal conversion.

The Form-Factor Challenge

During the transition to dual-monitor setups in enterprise and financial environments, low-profile and half-height graphics cards faced a physical limitation. A standard DVI port is physically too wide to fit two side-by-side connections on a single low-profile bracket. Manufacturers needed a way to output two high-resolution digital or analog signals without occupying two physical expansion slots.

The DMS-59 connector solved this spatial constraint. Measuring roughly the same width as a standard DVI port, it provided enough internal pin density to carry two full sets of video display signals concurrently.

Pinout and Signal Architecture

The connector houses 59 pins organized into four rows, with a single missing pin that serves as a keying mechanism to prevent incorrect insertion. To achieve dual DVI output, the graphics card’s display controller routes two entirely independent Single-Link DVI-I channels directly through this single interface.

A standard Single-Link DVI connection requires three Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) data pairs (one each for red, green, and blue) and one TMDS clock pair, alongside dedicated Display Data Channel (DDC) lines for Plug-and-Play monitor detection, hot-plug detection, ground, and +5V power. The DMS-59 pinout integrates two full sets of these requirements:

  • Channel 1: Four TMDS differential pairs (RGB + Clock), DDC clock and data, and dedicated ground shielding.
  • Channel 2: A completely separate second set of four TMDS differential pairs, DDC lines, and shielding.
  • Analog Support: Additional pins dedicated to analog RGB signals and horizontal/vertical sync, enabling backward compatibility with VGA displays using appropriate adapters.

Because both sets of lines are physically isolated within the 59-pin housing, the two channels operate simultaneously without signal crosstalk or bandwidth sharing.

Passive Breakout Mechanics

Unlike newer protocols that multiplex video streams through software or packetized data architectures, DMS-59 operates as a purely native physical pathway.

Graphics cards equipped with DMS-59, such as the NVIDIA Quadro NVS and ATI/AMD FireMV series, feature dual internal RAMDACs and digital display pipelines. The card outputs two standard, uncompressed video signals directly to the pin grid. A passive "Y-splitter" breakout cable is then connected to the DMS-59 port. This cable acts simply as a physical wire harness, mapping the 59 unified pins directly to two standard female DVI-I or DVI-D connectors.

Because the split is physical rather than digital, the computer’s operating system and display drivers recognize the setup as two distinct, hardware-level display outputs. Users can configure extended desktops or cloned displays with standard DVI resolutions up to 1920x1200 at 60 Hz per display, without needing active converters or external power sources.
