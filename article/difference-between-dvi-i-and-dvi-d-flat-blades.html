Difference Between DVI-I and DVI-D Flat Blades

Understanding the difference between a DVI-I flat blade and a DVI-D flat blade comes down to physical dimensions, pin configurations, and signal compatibility. While both connector types transmit digital video, DVI-I supports both digital and analog signals, requiring a wider flat blade surrounded by four extra pins. In contrast, DVI-D is purely digital, featuring a narrower flat blade with no surrounding pins to physically prevent users from connecting an incompatible analog source into a digital-only display.

Purpose of the Flat Blade

The horizontal flat blade—located on the side of the connector separate from the main pin grid—is designated as Pin C5. In analog transmission, this blade functions as the ground return for the red, green, and blue video signals, as well as the horizontal sync.

The DVI-I Flat Blade

DVI-I stands for "Digital Visual Interface - Integrated." It is designed to carry both digital and legacy analog (VGA-compatible) signals through a single cable.

  • Surrounding Pins: The DVI-I flat blade is surrounded by four small, round pins (labeled C1 through C4). These four pins carry the analog Red, Green, Blue, and Horizontal Sync signals.
  • Blade Dimensions: The flat blade on a DVI-I connector is wider than the blade on a DVI-D connector.
  • Signal Support: Because it contains the four analog pins and the full ground blade, it can transmit both high-resolution digital data and standard analog signals.

The DVI-D Flat Blade

DVI-D stands for "Digital Visual Interface - Digital." It is designed exclusively for digital displays, such as modern LCD monitors and digital projectors.

  • No Surrounding Pins: The DVI-D connector completely omits the four analog pins around the flat blade. The area around the blade is entirely empty.
  • Blade Dimensions: The flat blade on a DVI-D connector is noticeably narrower/thinner than the blade found on a DVI-I connector.
  • Signal Support: It carries digital signals only and cannot transmit analog video.

Cross-Compatibility and Key Differences

The difference in blade width and the presence of the four analog pins serve as a deliberate physical keying mechanism:

  • DVI-D Cable into a DVI-I Port: A male DVI-D cable will fit into a female DVI-I port. Because the DVI-D blade is narrower and lacks the four extra pins, the DVI-I port easily accommodates it and simply ignores the unused analog circuits.
  • DVI-I Cable into a DVI-D Port: A male DVI-I cable cannot be plugged into a female DVI-D port. The wider blade of the DVI-I cable physically will not enter the narrower slot of the DVI-D port, and the four surrounding analog pins will be blocked by the flat faceplate of the DVI-D connector. This prevents damage to the connector and stops users from attempting to feed analog signals into a device that can only read digital signals.
