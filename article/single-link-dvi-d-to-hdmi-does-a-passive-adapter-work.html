Single-Link DVI-D to HDMI: Does a Passive Adapter Work?

A passive adapter can successfully connect a single-link DVI-D port to an HDMI display. Because single-link DVI and HDMI share the exact same underlying electrical signaling for digital video, no active conversion hardware or external power is required. This guide explains how this compatibility works, the resolution limits of the connection, and key considerations regarding audio and directionality.

How Passive DVI-D to HDMI Compatibility Works

Single-link DVI-D and HDMI both transmit digital video using TMDS (Transition Minimized Differential Signaling). Because the signaling protocols are identical at the physical and electrical levels, a passive adapter only needs to physically reroute the pins from the DVI connector to the corresponding pins on the HDMI connector. There is no signal degradation, lag, or processing overhead involved in this connection.

Resolution and Refresh Rate Limits

While a passive connection works seamlessly, it is bound by the bandwidth limitations of single-link DVI:

  • Maximum Pixel Clock: 165 MHz
  • Standard Maximum Resolution: 1920×1080 (1080p) at 60Hz or 1920×1200 at 60Hz
  • Higher Refresh Rates: Lower resolutions (such as 720p) can support higher refresh rates, but modern high-bandwidth formats like 4K or 1440p at 144Hz exceed the single-link specification and cannot be achieved using a standard passive DVI adapter.

HDMI displays with high native resolutions will scale the 1080p signal automatically, though the output will remain limited to the maximum capabilities of the DVI-D port.

Audio Transmission Considerations

The original DVI specification was designed exclusively for video and does not officially support digital audio transmission. Consequently, in most cases, connecting a DVI-D source to an HDMI display using a passive adapter will only transmit the video feed, requiring a separate audio cable (such as a 3.5mm auxiliary cable) for sound.

However, certain modern graphics cards (primarily from AMD and NVIDIA) can embed standard HDMI audio signals over DVI pins. If the source hardware supports this non-standard feature, the passive adapter may carry both sound and video to the HDMI display. If the source does not support audio over DVI, the display will simply receive video without sound.

Bidirectional Functionality

Passive DVI-D to HDMI adapters and cables are fully bidirectional. You can use the exact same passive adapter to connect:

  1. A computer with a single-link DVI-D output to an HDMI monitor or television.
  2. A device with an HDMI output (such as a console, media player, or laptop) to a monitor with a DVI-D input.

In both directions, standard HDCP (High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection) digital handshake signals pass through uninterrupted, ensuring protected commercial content displays properly as long as both connected devices support the standard.
