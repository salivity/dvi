When Did GPUs Phase Out DVI Ports?

This article examines the timeline and technological shift surrounding the retirement of the Digital Visual Interface (DVI) connector on modern graphics cards. It covers the pivotal years when industry giants NVIDIA and AMD initiated the transition, the hardware generations that led the change, and the technical limitations that ultimately made DisplayPort and HDMI the preferred standards.

Major GPU manufacturers officially began phasing out native DVI ports from reference graphics cards between 2015 and 2016.

The groundwork for this transition was laid in December 2010, when a coalition of leading tech companies—including AMD, Intel, Dell, Lenovo, and Samsung—announced an industry-wide commitment to phase out legacy interfaces like VGA and DVI by 2015 in favor of DisplayPort and HDMI.

AMD took the first decisive step among consumer graphics cards in 2015 with the launch of the Radeon R9 Fury and Fury X, both of which entirely eliminated native DVI connections on reference models. The following year, AMD continued this trend by omitting DVI from the reference designs of the mainstream Radeon RX 480 series.

NVIDIA followed closely during the 2016–2017 hardware cycle. While base reference models of the GeForce GTX 1080 initially retained a single dual-link DVI port in early 2016, NVIDIA removed the connector entirely from the Titan X (Pascal) later that year and from the GeForce GTX 1080 Ti in early 2017. By the time the GeForce RTX 20-series arrived in 2018, native DVI ports had disappeared completely from high-end and mid-range reference platforms.

The primary driver behind phasing out DVI was bandwidth. Dual-link DVI was limited to resolutions of 2560x1600 at 60 Hz or 1080p at 144 Hz. As modern displays shifted toward 4K resolutions, higher refresh rates, variable refresh rate technologies (G-Sync and FreeSync), and HDR color, DVI could no longer keep pace.

Additionally, the bulky physical profile of the DVI port consumed critical space on modern GPU brackets, restricting internal airflow and limiting room for sleeker multi-port layouts dominated by DisplayPort and HDMI. While some budget third-party partner cards continued to include DVI ports for several years, major manufacturers had fully relegated the standard to legacy status by 2018.
