Professional CRT Monitors With DVI Inputs

During the twilight years of cathode-ray tube technology in the early 2000s, several high-end professional CRT monitors were produced with DVI-I connectors. While CRTs are fundamentally analog displays incapable of directly rendering a purely digital signal without internal conversion, manufacturers utilized DVI-I (Integrated) ports to take advantage of the format's dedicated analog pins—effectively utilizing the DVI-A (Analog) standard to deliver high-bandwidth, interference-free video from modern graphics cards.

How DVI Functioned on CRT Monitors

A standard DVI-I port contains both digital transmission pins and four separate analog pins flanking a flat ground blade. Because digital-to-analog converters (DACs) of sufficient quality and speed to match the high refresh rates and resolutions of professional CRTs were expensive and introduced latency, manufacturers did not decode the digital DVI-D signal inside the monitor.

Instead, these monitors wired the analog pins (C1 through C4, carrying red, green, blue, and horizontal sync) directly to the monitor's internal video processing board. This provided the user with a cleaner, better-shielded analog connection than traditional VGA cables, while maintaining direct compatibility with the DVI-I output ports that had become standard on professional workstation graphics cards from companies like ATI, NVIDIA, and Matrox.

Notable Professional Models with DVI-I

A select group of flagship CRT monitors included DVI-I inputs, frequently alongside standard 15-pin D-Sub (VGA) or 5-BNC connectors to support dual-system setups:

  • Eizo Nanao FlexScan Series: High-end models such as the 21-inch Eizo FlexScan T965 and T966 (built using Sony FD Trinitron apertures), as well as the 19-inch T766, featured a dual-input configuration consisting of one standard D-Sub input and one DVI-I input. These displays were widely used in color-critical photography, pre-press proofing, and medical imaging.
  • IBM P275: Another premier 21-inch Trinitron-based display, the IBM P275, offered dual inputs that included a standard VGA port and a DVI-I port. Users could toggle between two separate computer systems using a front-panel input select button.
  • Apple Studio Display CRT (ADC): Apple implemented a proprietary variant of DVI known as the Apple Display Connector (ADC) on its late-generation 17-inch transparent and graphite Studio Display CRTs. ADC combined DVI digital, DVI analog, USB, and monitor power into a single umbilical cable. The CRT version routed analog RGB lines through the ADC connector directly from the graphics card.

Why DVI-I Did Not Become the Standard for CRTs

The inclusion of DVI-I ports on professional CRT displays occurred at the very end of CRT manufacturing, primarily between 2001 and 2004. Shortly after these models were released, the rapid commercial maturation of liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) displaced CRTs in enterprise and design environments. Because LCDs natively required digital signaling via DVI-D, manufacturers directed their research and production toward flat-panel technology rather than updating remaining CRT chassis with hybrid connectors. As a result, DVI-equipped CRTs remained a rare, high-end niche reserved for premium workstation monitors.
