Using HDCP Strippers with DVI-D Sources

High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) frequently prevents protected video from displaying properly on older or non-compliant DVI-D monitors, capture cards, and projectors. Because DVI-D and HDMI share the same underlying digital video signaling (TMDS), users can integrate inexpensive HDMI-based HDCP splitters or "strippers" into a DVI-D video chain. This guide explains how HDCP affects DVI-D devices, the hardware required to bypass the handshake, and the exact step-by-step setup needed to restore video output.

Why HDCP Blocks DVI-D Displays

HDCP is an encryption protocol designed to prevent unauthorized copying of digital media. While many modern DVI-D inputs support HDCP, older computer monitors, broadcast gear, and legacy projectors often do not. When a source device—such as a PC, Blu-ray player, or gaming console—detects a display without valid HDCP keys, it either displays an error message, outputs static (snow), or disables the video signal entirely.

Required Hardware

Most dedicated HDCP removal devices on the market are built with standard HDMI ports rather than DVI ports. Because the digital video signals of HDMI and single-link DVI-D are electrically identical, you can use HDMI hardware with passive adapters. You will need:

  1. A DVI-D to HDMI Cable or Adapter: Used to connect your DVI-D video source to the stripper.
  2. An HDCP Stripper or Powered HDMI Splitter: Certain powered 1x2 HDMI splitters inadvertently or intentionally strip HDCP encryption. When the splitter negotiates with the source, it completes the HDCP handshake itself and outputs an unencrypted (HDCP-free) digital signal to all connected outputs.
  3. An HDMI to DVI-D Cable or Adapter: Used to connect the output of the splitter to your DVI-D display.
  4. External Power Supply: Passive adapters do not provide enough power to run the internal chipset of an active splitter. The splitter must be externally powered via USB or a dedicated DC adapter.

Step-by-Step Setup

  1. Connect the Source to the Splitter Input: Plug the DVI-D end of your first adapter cable into your source device's DVI-D port. Plug the HDMI end into the "Input" port of the powered splitter.
  2. Connect the Display to the Splitter Output: Connect an HDMI-to-DVI-D cable from "Output 1" of the splitter directly into your DVI-D display or capture card.
  3. Power On the Hardware: Plug in the external power supply for the splitter first to allow its internal firmware to boot. Then, turn on your DVI-D display, and finally, boot or wake the video source.
  4. Verify Resolution and Handshake: The source device should now detect an HDCP-compliant sink (the splitter). It will output the full digital signal, which the splitter passes to the DVI-D monitor without encryption.

Important Technical Considerations

  • Single-Link vs. Dual-Link DVI: Most HDMI splitters only support single-link DVI bandwidth. This limits output resolutions to 1920x1200 at 60Hz or 1080p at 60Hz. Higher refresh rates (such as 144Hz) or resolutions like 2560x1600 require dual-link DVI, which standard HDMI splitters do not support.
  • Audio Handling: Native DVI-D connections typically do not transmit audio. If your media source requires audio routing, you must split or extract the audio signal separately, as the HDMI-to-DVI-D output cable will only carry the video stream to the display.
  • Color Space Issues: DVI-D devices natively expect an RGB color space. If your source defaults to YCbCr output when connected through the HDMI splitter, colors may appear distorted (tinted green or purple). In this case, manually set the video source's output color format to RGB Full or RGB Limited.
