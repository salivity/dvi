Can DVI Carry Analog Component Video?

While standard DVI specifications do not natively support traditional YPbPr component video, DVI can carry analog video signals through specific variants like DVI-I and DVI-A. By default, the analog pins on a DVI connector transmit RGBHV (VGA-style) signals rather than standard three-cable component video. However, certain hardware devices allow component signals to be passed over these analog pins using passive breakout adapters, while others require active signal transcoders.

DVI Connector Types and Analog Capabilities

To understand how DVI handles analog signals, it is essential to distinguish between the three primary DVI formats:

  • DVI-D (Digital Only): Contains only digital pins and cannot output or receive any analog signal, including component video.
  • DVI-A (Analog Only): Rare format that only carries analog video.
  • DVI-I (Integrated): Combines digital and analog transmission. It features the four cross-blade pins on the side of the connector dedicated entirely to carrying analog signals.

Only DVI-I and DVI-A connectors physically have the wiring to handle analog video transmission.

RGBHV vs. YPbPr Component Video

The primary barrier to running analog component video through DVI is the signaling standard:

  • RGBHV: The official analog standard for DVI-I and DVI-A. It splits video into separate Red, Green, and Blue color channels, alongside separate Horizontal and Vertical sync channels (identical to standard VGA).
  • YPbPr (Component): Splits video into three channels: Luminance (Y, which includes sync), Blue-difference (Pb), and Red-difference (Pr).

Because DVI was designed around RGBHV color space and timing, standard DVI-compliant ports do not output or interpret YPbPr component video natively.

When Can DVI Carry Component Video?

Despite standard specifications, DVI can carry YPbPr component video in specific scenarios:

  1. Hardware-Enabled Video Cards and Projectors: Certain older graphics cards (such as select ATI Radeon and NVIDIA GeForce models) and select home theater projectors were designed with multi-format DVI-I ports. These devices can detect a DVI-to-RCA adapter and internally switch the DVI-I analog pins from RGBHV output to YPbPr component output. In this case, a passive DVI-to-Component breakout cable will work.
  2. Using an Active Converter: If the transmitting device strictly outputs digital DVI or standard RGBHV analog video, a passive cable will not work. In these cases, an active converter or transcoder is required to translate the digital DVI-D signal or analog RGBHV signal into an analog YPbPr component signal.

In summary, a standard DVI connection natively carries analog RGB, not component video. A DVI port can only carry analog component video if it is a DVI-I connector and the connected hardware specifically supports YPbPr transmission over its analog pins.
