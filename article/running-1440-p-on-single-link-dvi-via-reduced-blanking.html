Running 1440p on Single-Link DVI via Reduced Blanking

This article explains how modifying display timing parameters—specifically shrinking the vertical blanking porch—reduces the overall pixel clock frequency, enabling high-resolution 1440p (2560x1440) video transmission across bandwidth-constrained single-link DVI connections.

The Single-Link DVI Bandwidth Limit

Single-link DVI relies on three Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) data lanes. By standard specification, single-link DVI is capped at a 165 MHz pixel clock, translating to a maximum throughput of roughly 3.96 Gbps (or 4.95 Gbps at 10-bit line coding). Modern 1440p resolutions at standard timings typically require dual-link DVI or modern standards like HDMI and DisplayPort, because a standard 2560x1440 signal at 60 Hz demands a pixel clock of roughly 241.5 MHz—far exceeding the standard 165 MHz threshold.

The Role of Blanking Porches in Display Timings

A video signal transmits more than just the active pixels visible on the screen. Inherited from the era of Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) monitors, video signals include blanking intervals: periods during which no image data is rendered.

The vertical interval consists of:

  • Vertical Front Porch: The pause between the end of the active frame and the synchronization pulse.
  • Vertical Sync (V-Sync): The pulse instructing the display to prepare for the next frame.
  • Vertical Back Porch: The pause following synchronization before the next frame's active scanlines begin.

Together with the active vertical resolution (1440 lines), these blanking intervals define the "Total Vertical Lines" transmitted in each cycle.

How Reducing the Vertical Porch Lowers Bandwidth

The total bandwidth required by a video stream is dictated directly by the pixel clock, which is calculated as follows:

\[\text{Pixel Clock} = \text{Total Horizontal Pixels} \times \text{Total Vertical Lines} \times \text{Refresh Rate}\]

Because liquid crystal displays (LCDs) do not require physical electron guns to reposition themselves between frames, long blanking intervals are technically redundant.

By drastically reducing the vertical front and back porches (along with horizontal blanking intervals, commonly implemented via Coordinated Video Timings-Reduced Blanking, or CVT-RB):

  1. Total Frame Size Decreases: The stream eliminates dozens or hundreds of empty, non-data lines that would otherwise be calculated and transmitted per frame.
  2. Frequency Requirements Drop: Because fewer total lines must be sent per second to maintain the target refresh rate, the necessary pixel clock drops significantly toward the active pixel floor.
  3. Bandwidth Fits DVI Constraints: Minimizing the vertical blanking overhead lowers the data rate to a level that can either operate near the absolute single-link limit or fall within the real-world operational tolerance of modern single-link transmitters (which can often reach ~200–225 MHz without signal breakdown), or allow 1440p transmission at targeted refresh rates (such as 30 Hz to 45 Hz) directly within the strict 165 MHz specification.
